About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Language Technologies Institute, Carnegie Mellon University, where I am a member of the Multimodal Speech Processing (MSP) Lab led by Prof. Carlos Busso. My research sits at the intersection of multimodal speech processing and controllable generation: I model atypical speech and speech-grounded facial dynamics, and use generative models to make speech and facial technologies work for the people they most often fail, including children with speech disorders, individuals with craniofacial anomalies, and second-language learners.

My work spans generative reconstruction of disordered speech, synthetic data for low-resource clinical ASR, anatomically consistent facial analysis and synthesis, and interpretable multimodal biomarkers of speech disorders. In summer 2026 I interned with the Apple Accessibility team in Seattle, working on atypical speech generation.

My Ph.D. research is developed in collaboration with Children’s Health Dallas and UT Southwestern Medical Center, with Dr. Rami Hallac, Director of Research at the Analytical Imaging and Modeling (AIM) Center at Children’s Medical Center and Associate Professor at UT Southwestern. I communicate my work to both the AI and the medical communities: beyond AI venues such as ICMI and Interspeech, I have presented at clinical meetings, including the American Cleft Palate–Craniofacial Association annual meeting (ACPA 2024) and the International Congress on Cleft Lip/Palate (CLEFT 2025), and published in The Cleft Palate Craniofacial Journal.

Research

Multimodal and generative models of human communication that remain faithful to atypical anatomy and speech.

Generative modeling of atypical speech

Controllable TTS, voice conversion and flow-based models that synthesize or reconstruct disordered speech, so clinical ASR and assessment tools can be trained with minutes (not hours) of real patient data.

SLT 2026 Interspeech 2025

Anatomically consistent facial analysis & synthesis

Landmark detection, normalization and text-guided diffusion for faces with craniofacial anomalies, designed to preserve clinically meaningful anatomy rather than “correct” it away.

ICMI 2025 CLEFT 2025 CPCJ 2025 +2 more

Multimodal biomarkers of speech disorders

Aligning speech, text, facial asymmetry and articulatory motion into interpretable representations of severity, toward objective and explainable clinical evaluation.

Open-source releases accompanying my papers

SLT 2026

ChiReSSD

Speaker-preserving reconstruction of disordered child speech, built on StyleTTS2. Synthesizes a target transcript with canonical pronunciation in the child’s own voice.

ICMI 2026

MMDyS

Multimodal dysarthria severity assessment that aligns speech, text and facial asymmetry. Includes pretrained video, audio-text and fusion checkpoints.

CLEFT 2025

Anonymizing Faces, Preserving Anomalies

Privacy protection for clinical photos of patients with cleft lip. A diffusion model synthesizes a new face around the patient’s preserved orofacial region.
